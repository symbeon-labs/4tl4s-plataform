# Artifact Production

## Mission

Artifact Production governs the creation, revision, review, packaging, and delivery of discrete outputs. It is distinct from software engineering, although software projects may produce artifacts as part of their lifecycle.

## Artifact examples

- technical and research reports;
- specifications and architecture documents;
- datasets and data dictionaries;
- models, prompts, evaluation sets, and configuration packages;
- diagrams, design systems, visual assets, and manuals;
- training materials, proposals, and client deliverables;
- reproducible experiment packages and release documentation.

## Artifact record

Each artifact should carry:
- stable ID, title, type, and purpose;
- originating initiative and work package;
- owner, author, contributors, and reviewers;
- source materials and provenance;
- version and change history;
- sensitivity and access classification;
- IP classification and applicable license;
- quality criteria and review state;
- approval and acceptance records;
- delivery format, location, and retention policy.

## Lifecycle

briefed → in-production → review → revision → approved → released/delivered → superseded or archived.

Not every artifact requires every state. The applicable workflow and approval authority must be specified in its brief.

## Quality and acceptance

Quality criteria should be defined before production. Review should assess correctness, completeness, consistency, usability, accessibility where relevant, source integrity, and compliance with the brief. Client acceptance is recorded separately from internal approval.

## Version integrity

Never silently replace a released artifact. Create a new version, record material changes, and preserve the relationship to prior versions. For generated or transformed outputs, retain the relevant inputs and process metadata when lawful and practical.
