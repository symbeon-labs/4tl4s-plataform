# Applied Research

## Mission

Applied Research investigates questions with practical technological, scientific, methodological, or organizational relevance. Its output is evidence-backed knowledge—not necessarily a working product.

## Suitable outputs

- validated, weakened, or rejected hypotheses;
- research protocols and methods;
- experiment records and reproducible analyses;
- datasets with provenance and rights metadata;
- technical reports and literature reviews;
- architectures, models, and protocols at a stated maturity;
- negative results and documented limitations.

## Research record

A research initiative should define:
1. question and context;
2. hypothesis or research objective;
3. prior knowledge and source register;
4. methodology and experimental design;
5. data sources, rights, and limitations;
6. expected observations and evaluation criteria;
7. responsible researcher and review method;
8. results, uncertainty, reproducibility, and limitations;
9. resulting artifacts and proposed next steps.

## Research status

Suggested statuses: proposed, qualified, approved, active, under-review, completed, inconclusive, rejected, archived.

Status is not a maturity score. Maturity and confidence should be separately described with their evidence basis.

## Research integrity

- Preserve original observations and source references.
- Separate observed results from interpretation.
- Record failed experiments and negative findings.
- Identify AI assistance and generated material where relevant.
- Do not overstate statistical, causal, or generalization claims.
- Respect dataset licenses, consent, privacy, and research restrictions.

## Transfer to development

A research output may enter a transfer review when there is a clear receiving use case. The review must assess evidence quality, reproducibility, limitations, feasibility, security and safety implications, IP provenance, third-party restrictions, and proposed engineering acceptance criteria.

The receiving development initiative remains independently scoped and owned. A research result does not become production-ready merely because a transfer is approved.

## Non-goals

The research workspace is not a substitute for peer review, institutional ethics review, legal review, or domain-specific regulatory approval where those are required.
